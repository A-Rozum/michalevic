What if an international search violates your rights?

Start by assessing the circumstances and planning a defence. As new information emerges and the case develops, the plan can be adjusted.

The office assists people who believe an international search against them is unfounded or politically motivated. A search can affect several countries at once, so steps in each country need to fit into an overall strategy.

How can you prepare before an arrest?

Start by assessing what is known about the allegation and the search. Gather the official information available: about data held by INTERPOL through the applicable procedures, about the case in the country that initiated the search, and about possible action in the country where you are staying. Access to case materials depends on the stage of proceedings and the rules in each country.

That information can help establish a defence position and a sequence of steps. One possible route is a request to the Commission for the Control of INTERPOL’s Files (CCF), which handles requests about personal data in INTERPOL’s files. Article 3 of INTERPOL’s Constitution prohibits the organisation from undertaking activities of a political, military, religious or racial character. Whether these mechanisms apply depends on the particular case.

What happens if you are detained?

Representation in the courts of the country of detention requires a lawyer authorised to practise there. The office can work with that lawyer to develop a common defence position, prepare materials on the case and, where appropriate and permitted by the procedure, give evidence as an expert or witness. The person detained, their relatives or other representatives can contact the office.

What else can an international search affect?

It can complicate applications for residence or citizenship, obtaining a visa while staying in a third country where it is unsafe to remain, and access to property or funds abroad. These questions belong in the overall strategy and need to be addressed under the rules of the countries concerned.

What should you tell a lawyer?

  • Which country initiated the search?
  • Do you know what you are accused of?
  • Can you access the case materials?
  • Have any pre-trial restrictions been imposed?
  • Is your status in the country where you are staying regularised?

The office can provide a detailed list of documents needed to prepare your legal defence.
